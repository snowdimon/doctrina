About the Doctrine

The Doctrine of Systems Design is not a collection of abstract rules. It is a map for an Architect whose planning horizon exceeds a human life.

The author of the Doctrine, Dmitry Snezhkov (Snowdimon), spent ten years in methodology and business process implementation within a major Russian corporation. He saw how ideas are born and how they die. How best practices, when mishandled, lead to catastrophe. How systems become zombies, and how the best employees burn out first.

From this experience and reflections on the nature of long-term systems, a doctrine was born that answers the question: how to build so that your creation outlives you and does not become a burden to your heirs.

Six Principles

Each principle is an independent work with its own metrics and protocols. All of them are open under the CC BY-SA 4.0 license.

Principium

The Beginning. Choose a Cathedral larger than your life.

Anti-Icarus

A visionary's viability protocol. Self-care is a strategic necessity.

Absurdum ad Veritatem

Test an idea by taking it to its ultimate embodiment. If the system breaks, the flaw was in it from the start.

Shadow Co-Author

Pass on not only the blueprints, but the spirit of the system. Explicit knowledge is a manual. Tacit knowledge is a legacy.

Dignified Decommissioning

Design a system's right to its own end. Immortality without purpose is a threat.

Compatibility Lexicon

Learn to interface with other systems without losing your identity.

For Whom

  • Visionaries and Futurists If your planning horizon is 30 years or more, the Doctrine will give you a language to describe your path and tools to avoid burning out on it.
  • Engineers and Leaders If you design complex systems and want them not to become «zombies» in ten years, you will find methodologies for verification and transmission here.
  • Parents and Mentors Parenthood is a system with a 60-year horizon. The Doctrine explains how to raise a person capable of building their own Cathedrals, not just maintaining yours.

Applied Projects

IPIS / Duranki — Interplanetary Identity System. An open protocol designed as the Doctrine's canonical case study. It has passed the Kafka Test: its ultimate embodiment in a world of colonial segregation did not destroy the intent, but revealed its deepest truth.

The Duranki Trilogy — fiction books that guide the reader into the ethics of systems design through the narrative of a dystopia. Available on Author.Today.
