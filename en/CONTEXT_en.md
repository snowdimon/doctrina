## Interdisciplinary Context of the Doctrine of Systems Design

The Doctrine of Systems Design is not merely a collection of principles, but an integrated teaching that translates fundamental problems of cybernetics, philosophy, and psychology into practical protocols and metrics.

### Cybernetics & Systems Theory
The Doctrine addresses three key problems that stopped both classical and second-order cybernetics:
- **The observer problem (H. von Foerster).** The observer is inseparable from the system. The Doctrine makes their state a manageable parameter through the Anti-Icarus principle with Visionary Viability Metrics (VVM).
- **The problem of transferring tacit knowledge (M. Polanyi).** How to preserve non-formalized information that lives only in the creator's experience? The Shadow Co-Author principle provides protocols for transmitting the «spirit» of the system: context, doubts, and values.
- **The problem of zombie-systems and the local maximum trap.** Systems optimized for old conditions continue to consume resources. The Dignified Decommissioning principle introduces the system's right to a dignified end as a standard architectural mode.

Additionally, Absurdum ad Veritatem offers a method for testing for inherent flaws, and the Compatibility Lexicon provides a protocol for interfacing systems with different ontologies without unification.

### Philosophy
The Doctrine addresses fundamental existential and ethical questions:
- **Meaning in the face of death.** Principium translates the fear of finitude into constructive action, offering the concept of the «Cathedral» — a goal larger than a single human life.
- **Ethics of finitude.** Dignified Decommissioning asserts a system's right to its own end, opposing the cult of endless growth. This echoes Stoicism and *memento mori*.
- **The problem of the Other.** The Compatibility Lexicon solves the problem of interaction between different value systems without mutual destruction or absorption, based on respect for «red lines» and a dialogue protocol.
- **The nature of evil and creation.** The Doctrine demonstrates that creative systems are architecturally more durable than destructive ones, not for moral reasons, but structural ones.

### Psychology
The Doctrine offers systemic solutions for a wide range of psychological problems associated with long-term creation:
- **Visionary burnout and isolation.** Anti-Icarus provides preventive protocols: a grounding circuit, a heresy inoculation, an ethical switch.
- **Cognitive biases and rigidity.** The Kafka Test allows the identification of unconscious destructive rules and the addition of safeguards.
- **Fear of loss and letting go.** Dignified Decommissioning helps to design healthy separation (in personal relationships, careers, projects), turning completion from a catastrophe into an act of experience extraction.
- **Trauma of losing a knowledge carrier.** The Shadow Co-Author reduces anxiety by documenting doubts and preparing living carriers of the system's spirit.

### Uniqueness of the Doctrine
The Doctrine of Systems Design is not a philosophical treatise, an engineering standard, or a psychological methodology taken separately. It is an **interdisciplinary bridge** that turns «eternal questions» into measurable parameters and working protocols applicable in any sphere — from space programs to raising children.
